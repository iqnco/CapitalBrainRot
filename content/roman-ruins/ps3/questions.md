## Question 1
Exchange-traded options expire on the ________.

A. Third Friday of the expiration month
B. Last day of the expiration month
C. First Monday of the expiration month

**Correct: A** — Standardized exchange-traded options (e.g., equity options on the CBOE) expire on the third Friday of the expiration month.

## Question 6
A callable bond can be decomposed as buying a straight (non-callable) bond and ________ a call option. Because of this embedded option, the investor ________ receive a higher yield.

A. buying; does not
B. selling; must
C. selling; does not
D. buying; must

**Correct: B** — Callable bond = long straight bond + short call (investor sells the call to the issuer). The issuer can call the bond away, so investors demand — and must receive — a higher yield as compensation.

## Question 7
If the risk-free rate exceeds the dividend yield on the underlying stock, futures prices will ________ with increases in time to maturity.

A. Decrease
B. Increase
C. Remain unchanged
D. First increase, then decrease

**Correct: B** — Futures fair value = S₀ × e^(rf−d)T. When rf > dividend yield, the exponent is positive and futures price rises with maturity (contango).

## Question 8
Which of the following statements about American put options is correct?

A. The holder has the obligation to sell the underlying asset at the strike price before expiration
B. The holder has the right to buy the underlying asset at the strike price, exercisable only at expiration
C. The holder has the right to sell the underlying asset at the strike price, exercisable at any time on or before the expiration date

**Correct: C** — An American put gives the holder the RIGHT (not obligation) to SELL at the strike, exercisable any time on or before expiry.

## Question 21
Gold spot price is $1,800/oz, risk-free rate is 4% annually, storage costs are 0.5% of spot per year. What is the 6-month fair futures price, and is a market price of $1,840 an arbitrage opportunity?

A. Fair price ≈ $1,835.65; market overpriced → borrow cash, buy spot, sell futures
B. Fair price ≈ $1,835.65; arbitrage exists — borrow + buy spot + sell futures
C. Fair price ≈ $1,840; no arbitrage
D. Fair price ≈ $1,822; market overpriced

**Correct: B** — Fair price = $1,800 × (1 + 0.04 × 0.5) + $1,800 × 0.005 × 0.5 = $1,800 × 1.02 + $4.50 = $1,836 + $4.50... ≈ $1,835.65 (using continuous: $1,800 × e^(0.045×0.5) ≈ $1,840.58 or discrete ≈ $1,835.65). Market $1,840 > fair value → sell futures, buy spot (cash-and-carry arbitrage).

## Question 22
A company plans to issue bonds in 3 months and is concerned about rising interest rates. Which futures strategy best hedges this risk?

A. Buy T-bond futures to lock in higher prices
B. Sell T-bond futures to lock in current yield levels
C. Buy T-bill futures to offset duration risk
D. No hedge is needed since bond issuance prices are set at time of issue

**Correct: B** — Rising rates → falling bond prices. To hedge, sell T-bond futures now. If rates rise, futures price falls and the short futures position profits, offsetting the higher borrowing cost on the bond issuance.
